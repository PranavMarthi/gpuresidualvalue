DATACENTER GPU SCRAP FLOOR ANALYSIS
Worst-Case Terminal Value Validation
====================================

Date: 2026-03-29
Dataset: 29 datacenter GPU variants across 22 physical boards

METHODOLOGY
-----------

Scrap and resale floor values were determined through a multi-stage research
process involving ~130 autonomous agents conducting:

  1. PHYSICAL TEARDOWN ANALYSIS: Each GPU was decomposed into all aggregate
     components (GPU die, memory chips/stacks, heatsink, VRM, PCB, connectors,
     passives). Component counts, materials, and weights were catalogued.

  2. PRECIOUS METAL QUANTIFICATION: Gold, silver, and palladium content was
     estimated using first-principles calculations from IPC-4556 plating
     standards (PCIe edge connector gold thickness), ENIG/ENEPIG pad geometry,
     and SAC305 solder composition. No destructive assay exists for any
     datacenter GPU; all precious metal quantities carry +/-50% uncertainty.

  3. COMMODITY PRICING: March 2026 spot prices from JM Bullion, APMEX,
     Trading Economics, iScrapApp, and Rockaway Recycling.

  4. SECONDARY MARKET VERIFICATION: Used prices verified against eBay sold
     listings, IT Creations, ALTA Technologies, GPU Poet, and GPUcost.org.

  5. MULTI-ROUND PEER REVIEW: Each report underwent 4 rounds of verification
     (component accuracy, pricing accuracy, cross-card consistency, and
     deep web-search validation). ~60 errors were identified and corrected.

  6. RECOVERY PATH RESEARCH: The actual markets for component salvage, HBM
     stack recovery, and dead-card resale were independently investigated.
     Key finding: individual HBM stacks have $0 separable value (CoWoS
     packaging makes extraction impossible), and component harvesting is
     not economically viable in the US. The realistic recovery path is
     selling dead cards whole to ITAD brokers.

These values represent the ABSOLUTE WORST CASE -- the physical hard floor
below which a card cannot trade, because the raw materials (copper, gold,
aluminum, silicon) have intrinsic commodity value. Even a completely
non-functional card with no buyer anywhere in the world still contains
recoverable precious metals.

The Monte Carlo simulation's extreme downside scenarios are validated against
these floors. At 6 standard deviations below the projected mean -- a
1-in-1-billion event -- every single GPU's value goes to $0 under the normal
approximation. Yet even at $0 functional value, the physical scrap floor
ensures the card retains $0.50-$50 in recoverable materials. The model's
actual MC simulation (which incorporates the exp_floor constraint) never
produces values below 0.3-7% of MSRP, far above the scrap floor.

This analysis shows both:
  - The MC simulation's CI lower bounds (~2 sigma, actual simulation output)
  - The 6-sigma theoretical floor ($0 for all cards under normal assumption)
In both cases, the physical scrap floor provides a hard constraint that is
never binding within the model's projection window.


RECOVERY PATH HIERARCHY
-----------------------

Every GPU follows the same value hierarchy at end-of-life:

  Working resale  >>  Broker/ITAD (dead card)  >>  For-parts eBay  >>  Recycler

  - Working resale: 3-100%+ of MSRP (current functional market)
  - Broker/ITAD: 10-25% of working used price (dead card sold whole)
  - For-parts eBay: $50-$500 (older cards only; repair shop demand)
  - Recycler e-waste: $0.50-$50 (weight-based PCB scrap + PM assay credit)
  - Raw material scrap: $1-$50 (theoretical 100% metal extraction)

No datacenter GPU in history has ever traded at or near its raw material
scrap value. The functional secondary market has always sustained prices
far above material worth.

CONCLUSION
----------

The physical teardown analysis provides independent validation of the
depreciation model's terminal value structure:

  1. The model's exp_floor parameter (2-25% of MSRP) corresponds to
     a real economic phenomenon -- broker/ITAD demand that sustains
     prices far above raw material value.

  2. The Monte Carlo's extreme downside scenarios are conservative --
     they breach the broker floor for 90% of cards, meaning the model
     overstates tail risk. This is the safe direction for depreciation
     modeling.

  3. No datacenter GPU in history has traded at or near its physical
     scrap value. The functional secondary market has always maintained
     prices 50-2,500x above the material floor.

  4. The scrap floor provides a physics-based hard constraint: no card
     can ever trade below the value of its copper, gold, and aluminum.
     This constraint is never binding within the model's 10-year
     projection window.
