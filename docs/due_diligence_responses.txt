DUE DILIGENCE RESPONSES
GPU Secondary Market Depreciation Analysis
March 2026


RESIDUAL VALUE DATA & METHODOLOGY

Dataset:
 The depreciation model is built on approximately 300,000 cleaned price observations covering 60 GPU models from an initial dataset of 8.7 million. The primary paid dataset is three years of retail listing data from Silicon Data, covering June 2024 through March 2026 with approximately 158,000 US-only observations after cleaning. This is extended by Keepa historical price tracking going back to each GPU's original launch date -- critical for datacenter GPUs like the T4, which has 77 months of continuous history and serves as the primary anchor for the datacenter segment canonical depreciation curve. Supplementary pipelines fill gaps in datacenter coverage where enterprise GPUs lack retail history: completed secondary-market sales for 15 thin-data datacenter GPUs (H100, H200, MI300X, Gaudi2, etc.), web-scraped enterprise reseller data, and specification cross-checks against manufacturer databases. Datacenter GPU coverage remains the weakest area -- most rely on the 22-month Silicon Data window plus supplements, compared to 30-66 months for consumer models. Projections extend to 120 months at 12-month intervals, with the primary ranking metric at the 60-month mark.


Executed Transactions vs Indicative Pricing:
 The pipeline does not use any third-party indicative pricing model, consensus estimate, or model-derived valuation as an input. A dedicated filter removes any data rows flagged as estimates, forecasts, implied values, or consensus pricing to prevent circular dependency. The dataset draws a clear line between two tiers of pricing evidence: completed secondary-market sales (confirmed transactions where a buyer paid a specific price for a specific GPU) and retail marketplace listings (offered prices on major e-commerce platforms, which closely approximate executable prices given the high sell-through rates on those platforms but are not confirmed sales). Cloud rental rates and implied asset values derived from rental economics are excluded from curve fitting entirely -- rental rates reflect operating economics and provider margins, not the price a buyer would pay to own the underlying hardware.


Back-Testing and Validation:
 The model has been validated via temporal holdout on four GPUs with sufficient history -- all four actual values fell within their 95% credible intervals (T4: projected 0.159 vs actual 0.153, +3.9% error; RTX 3090: projected 1.13 vs actual 1.13, +0.7% error; V100 PCIe 32 GB and A10 both within CI). For datacenter GPUs where the five-year mark has not yet been reached, a hierarchical Bayesian model implemented produces independent estimates with a 0.948 Pearson correlation against the primary pipeline, confirming robustness. A sensitivity analysis varying four key pipeline thresholds across their plausible ranges shows the depreciation ranking is stable (Spearman rho > 0.965 for every scenario). The most commercially important finding for datacenter GPUs: the retail-only data systematically overstates value retention -- when supplementary data is added, all ten largest TVR revisions are downward, with the H200 NVL dropping from 97% to 35% retention and the H100 SXM5 64 GB from 59% to 18%.


SECONDARY MARKET & LIQUIDITY
 Datacenter GPUs trade through IT asset disposition firms, specialist hardware brokers, online secondary-market platforms, and direct enterprise-to-enterprise channels, with transaction sizes ranging from single units to multi-GPU server trays. Current-generation accelerators trade at multiples of MSRP reflecting AI compute scarcity, while previous-generation datacenter GPUs show steep depreciation -- older models retain as little as 3-15% of MSRP after 7-9 years, and mid-lifecycle models sit at roughly 35-50% depending on form factor and remaining demand for the architecture. The datacenter secondary market is the least transparent segment, with limited public price discovery compared to consumer hardware. The certified refurbishment premium of +19.4% over used condition (p < 0.0001, 95% CI: +14.9% to +24.1%) is relevant for datacenter remarketing -- some datacenter cards show premiums exceeding 40%, indicating meaningful value uplift from certified refurbishment in this segment. Observation density in the pipeline serves as a proxy for market depth: datacenter GPUs with fewer observations across all sources have thinner markets.
