MONTE CARLO SIMULATION METHODOLOGY
GPU Secondary Market Depreciation Analysis
March 2026


1. INPUTS

The MC takes the outputs of the curve-fitting stage:

  - Fitted model parameters (floor, decay rate k or exponent b)
  - Parameter covariance matrix, which captures how uncertain each
    parameter is and how they correlate with each other
  - Segment-level canonical parameters — a pooled fit across well-observed
    GPUs in each market segment (datacenter, workstation, consumer)
  - Current market price ratio for each GPU (latest observed price / MSRP)

For Tier 2 GPUs, the floor parameter is regularised toward a segment-level
empirical prior before entering the MC. This borrows strength from mature
GPUs in the same segment to inform the projection for newer ones.


2. HOW IT WORKS

  10,000 draws per GPU, validated by comparing against 2,000-draw runs
  and confirming CI estimates were stable to within 1%.

  The sampling approach follows a three-tier hierarchy based on data
  availability:

  TIER 1 — Ensemble (individually fitted GPUs):
    Two candidate models (exponential-with-floor, power-law-with-floor)
    are blended using Akaike weights from their AICc scores. Per draw:
      1. Sample parameters from a multivariate normal centred on the
         fitted values, using the covariance matrix
      2. Clip to physically plausible bounds
      3. Evaluate each model at target time points (every 12 months out
         to 10 years)
      4. Blend using the Akaike weights

  TIER 2 — Constrained (segment-informed GPUs):
    The decay rate k is anchored from the segment-level fit during the
    fitting step; the floor is fitted to the individual GPU's data. In
    the MC, both floor and k are sampled jointly. Per draw:
      1. Sample [floor, k] jointly from a 2D normal using the fitted
         floor value plus the cross-covariance from the segment fit
         (floor and k are negatively correlated — a faster decay implies
         a lower floor to match the same data)
      2. Clip to bounds and evaluate the exponential model
      3. Scale to the GPU's current market price, with per-draw noise
         to reflect uncertainty in the current price level

  TIER 3 — Segment Default (segment-driven GPUs):
    Same joint [floor, k] sampling and stochastic scaling as Tier 2,
    but parameters come entirely from the segment canonical fit.

  The covariance matrix from curve fitting assumes each data point is
  independent, but monthly GPU prices are autocorrelated. A Bartlett
  correction inflates the covariance by (1 + rho) / (1 - rho), where
  rho is the lag-1 autocorrelation, to account for this.


3. OUTPUTS

From the 10,000 x 11 draw matrix (one row per draw, columns at 0, 12,
24, 36, 48, 60, 72, 84, 96, 108, and 120 months from launch):

  - Point estimate: median of draws at the 60-month (5-year) mark
  - 95% confidence interval (HPD) at each time point — the narrowest
    interval containing 95% of draws
  - Standard deviation at each time point
  - Tail risk: fraction of draws below 10% of MSRP at the 5yr, 8yr,
    and 10yr marks
  - Full depreciation curve: median at each of the 11 time points
  - Per-time-point HPD bounds and std devs for reconstructing the full
    uncertainty fan


4. VALIDATION

  - Convergence test: compared 2,000 vs 10,000 draws on five GPUs (one
    per fitting pathway), confirmed stability at 10,000.

  - Temporal holdout: back-tested on four GPUs with long enough history
    to compare projections to actuals. All four fell within their 95%
    intervals.

  - Hierarchical Bayesian cross-check: a separate PyMC model produced
    independent value estimates with 0.948 correlation against the
    primary pipeline.

  - Sensitivity analysis: varied four key pipeline thresholds across
    their plausible ranges. Rankings were stable in every scenario
    (Spearman rho > 0.965).


5. WHY THESE CHOICES

  - Parametric MC (sampling parameters) rather than residual bootstrap:
    we need to project beyond the observed data window, and parametric
    sampling keeps trajectories physically plausible.

  - Joint [floor, k] sampling for Tier 2/3: these parameters are
    negatively correlated in the exponential model. Joint sampling
    preserves that structure and produces well-calibrated uncertainty.

  - HPD intervals rather than equal-tailed: shorter intervals when the
    distribution is skewed, which it often is near parameter bounds.
