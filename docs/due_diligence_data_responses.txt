1b. Sensitivity to accelerated depreciation
--------------------------------------------

If technology replacement cycles are 20% or 40% shorter than modelled
(equivalent to reading the model's Year 6 or Year 7 projection as the
"effective Year 5"), the impact is:

  Scenario                    Mean Yr5 Value    Cards Below 10%
  ----------------------------------------------------------
  Base case (Year 5)           44.7% of MSRP       0 of 29
  20% accelerated (eff. Yr6)   37.7% of MSRP       0 of 29
  40% accelerated (eff. Yr7)   32.6% of MSRP       0 of 29

Even under 40% accelerated depreciation, no card's central projection
falls below 10% of MSRP at the 5-year horizon. The lowest individual
card under the 40% stress scenario is V100 SXM2 16GB at 10.4% -- still
above the 10% floor.

The model's volatility (mean sigma = 19.0% at Year 5) already incorporates
substantial uncertainty about technology cycle timing. The Monte Carlo
paths implicitly sample scenarios where depreciation is faster or slower
than the fitted curve.

======================================

2a. Data sources and volume
---------------------------

The datacenter GPU dataset comprises ~104,000 price observations across
multiple sources (filtered to the model's 29 DATACENTER-segment GPUs only).
The critical distinction is between SOLD transaction data (what buyers
actually paid) and LISTING data (what sellers asked).

  Sold transactions (Tier 1):

    Keepa Amazon price tracking             94     Monthly actual sale prices tracked

    Scraped supplement (transactions)       56     eBay/IT Creations/ASA Computers
                                                   executed prices (DC GPUs only)
  Subtotal Tier 1 (transactions):          350     (0.3% of total)

  Listings & asking prices (Tier 2):
    Bloomberg/eBay listing scrape      103,132     Daily eBay ASKING prices 2024-2026,
                                                   scraped across all DC GPU categories.
                                                   These are what sellers list, NOT what
                                                   buyers pay. Systematically overstates
                                                   executable value by 15-30%.
    Cloud pricing (implied GPU value)      252     Derived from cloud rental rates
    Scraped supplement (estimates)           59     Analyst estimates, market ranges
    Web scraped supplement                  27     Retail listing price scrapes
    Vast.ai implied values                   8     GPU cloud marketplace rates
  Subtotal Tier 2 (listings/estimates):103,478     (99.7% of total)

  Total DC GPU data points:           103,828

The dataset is dominated by Tier 2 listing data (99.7%) which provides
dense time-series coverage for curve fitting. However, only 350 data
points (0.3%) represent verified executed transactions.

LISTING BIAS DISCLOSURE:

The dominance of listing (asking price) data is a material consideration.
Listing prices systematically overstate executable values by 15-30% for
datacenter GPUs, based on observed listing-to-sale discounts.

The model does NOT explicitly correct for listing bias. Specifically:

  1. CURVE FITTING: The model uses weighted least squares with sigma_i =
     max(y_i, 0.05), which weights observations proportionally to their
     price level. This gives lower-priced observations more influence
     (in relative terms), but does NOT specifically downweight listings
     vs transactions — all data points at a given price level receive
     equal treatment regardless of whether they represent a sold
     transaction or an unsold listing.

  2. TRANSACTION DATA: The 350 verified transaction points are mixed into
     monthly median time series alongside the 103K listing points. When
     both exist for the same GPU-month, eBay sold data takes priority
     via a deduplication step. However, for the majority of GPU-months
     where only listing data exists, the listing prices feed directly
     into curve fitting with no discount applied.

  3. VOLATILITY: The Monte Carlo sigma reflects parameter estimation
     uncertainty (how confident the model is in its fitted curve
     parameters), propagated through the covariance matrix. Noisy listing
     data indirectly widens this uncertainty, making the confidence
     intervals wider than they would be with cleaner data. This is a
     conservative effect for tail risk analysis.

  IMPLICATION: The model's central projections likely overstate true
  secondary market clearing prices by some portion of the 15-30%
  listing premium. This means the breach probabilities in Section 1
  may be slightly understated. However, because the model fits to
  monthly medians (not means), extreme listing outliers are partially
  filtered. The net listing bias in the fitted curves is likely smaller
  than the raw 15-30% listing premium but is not precisely quantified.

Note: "Tier 1/Tier 2" here refers to data source quality (transactions vs
listings), not the model's internal fitting tier classification (which
refers to how many observations each GPU has for curve fitting).

2b. Listing vs transaction price differences
---------------------------------------------

Systematic differences are observed in the scraped supplement (115 DC GPU
data points with confidence grades; the 103K Bloomberg/eBay listing data
does not carry confidence tags):

  - HIGH confidence (primarily eBay sold): 46 points, mean $19,325
  - MEDIUM confidence (mix of listings + sold): 50 points, mean $23,250
  - LOW confidence (estimates, implied values): 19 points, mean $14,409

The MEDIUM confidence data skews higher because it includes more new/retail
listing prices, while HIGH confidence data includes more used/secondary
market sold transactions. LOW confidence data includes implied cloud values
(Vast.ai) which reflect utilisation-adjusted pricing and tend to be lower.

The 103K Bloomberg/eBay listing prices do not have confidence grades but
are known to be asking prices (not sold). Industry data suggests listing-
to-execution discounts of 15-30% for datacenter GPUs.

The model applies proportional error weighting during curve fitting, which
gives natural weight to observations closer to the fitted curve. Higher
confidence observations tend to cluster more tightly, receiving implicit
(though not explicit) upweighting.

DATA LIMITATION — H100/H200 TRANSACTION DEPTH:

Verified sold transaction data for current-generation GPUs is thin
relative to the installed base. The H100 SXM5 family has 6 verified
sold records (4x 80GB, 1x 64GB, 1x 94GB), and the H200 family has 5
verified sold records (4x H200 NVL at $28,999-$32,998 and 1x H200 SXM
at $18,000). While non-zero, these sample sizes carry meaningful
uncertainty. H100/H200 inventory is predominantly held under primary
lease or hyperscaler deployment, limiting secondary market turnover.

The Bloomberg/eBay listing scrape provides ~8,600 H100-family and ~200
H200 listing data points for curve fitting, but these are asking prices
subject to the 15-30% listing bias discussed in Section 2. As H200
secondary supply increases through 2026-2027, transaction data density
will improve. For now, H100/H200 projections carry higher uncertainty
than older GPUs with deeper transaction histories (e.g., T4 with 26
sold records, A100 SXM4 with 20).

Typical ticket sizes: $700-$32,000 per unit. Enterprise lots (10+ units)
trade through brokers (ALTA Technologies, Net Equity) at 5-15% discount
to eBay retail.

Time-to-sale observations (estimated from listing duration and market liquidity;
not based on tracked transaction data):
  - H100/H200: 1-7 days (high demand, liquid market)
  - A100: 3-14 days (established market, moderate volume)
  - V100/T4: 7-30 days (declining demand, price-sensitive buyers)

3b. Stressed monetisation scenario (title transfer)
----------------------------------------------------

Under stressed conditions (forced liquidation, no warranty, bulk lot):

  Recovery range:    40-65% of prevailing secondary market price (estimate
                     based on ITAD broker market norms; not derived from
                     project transaction data)
  Timeline:          30-90 days for full lot disposition
  Channel:           ITAD broker (ALTA Technologies, Net Equity, IT Creations)
  Minimum lot value: Brokers typically require >$10,000 aggregate

  For a dead/non-functional card:
    Broker payout:   10-25% of working used price
    Recycler payout: $0.50-$50 per card (weight-based PCB scrap)
    Timeline:        7-21 days to recycler; 30-60 days to broker

  Example: 100x H100 SXM5 80GB portfolio
    Working value:   $960K-$1.5M (at $9,600-$15,000 each)
    Stressed sale:   $384K-$975K (40-65% recovery)
    Dead card sale:  $96K-$375K (broker at 10-25% of working)
    Scrap value:     $1,500-$3,200 (recycler at $15-$32 each)
    Timeline:        30-60 days (broker channel)


4. OPERATIONAL CONTROLS & RMA PROCESS
======================================

These questions relate to operational frameworks that sit outside the
depreciation model's scope. The model captures market-observable pricing
outcomes but does not model OEM-specific RMA workflows, configuration
tracking, or operating parameter compliance.

Observations from the data:
  - Cards traded as "used/tested" on eBay command 15-40% premium over
    "used/untested" or "as-is" listings, suggesting buyer-side quality
    verification is valued but not standardised
  - No datacenter GPU variant in the dataset shows pricing discontinuities
    attributable to warranty expiry or recertification events
  - The model's carrier-adjusted flag (is_carrier_adjusted) accounts for
    some condition-based pricing differences but does not distinguish
    OEM-warranted vs. grey-market inventory

5b. Portfolio expected loss (equal-weighted, all 29 DC GPUs)
------------------------------------------------------------

  Portfolio expected loss:     0.080 percentage points of MSRP
  As fraction of 10% floor:   0.79%

  Interpretation: For every $10M of notional exposure at the 10% floor,
  the expected loss is approximately $8,000.

  This is an extremely thin expected loss, driven by:
    - Only 11 of 29 GPUs have p(breach) > 1%
    - The highest-risk cards (V100 family) have low absolute MSRP ($10-$12K),
      limiting dollar-weighted impact in a diversified portfolio
    - The conditional shortfall is capped by the physical scrap floor
      (0.02-1% of MSRP), meaning losses cannot exceed ~9.8 pp even in
      the worst case (10% floor minus 0.2% scrap floor)

5c. Risk concentration
----------------------

  V100-family cards contribute 71% of total portfolio expected loss
  despite being only 17% of the card count (5 of 29 variants).

  Excluding V100-era cards (non-V100 contribution to the 29-GPU portfolio):
    E[L] contribution: 0.023 pp of MSRP (0.23% of floor)
    On $10M notional: $2,300 of the $8,000 total E[L]
    (Note: if recomputed as a standalone 24-GPU ex-V100 portfolio,
    the per-GPU E[L] would be 0.028 pp / $2,800 on $10M)

  This suggests that for a modern datacenter GPU portfolio (Ampere generation
  and newer), the 10% floor structure has negligible expected loss.
